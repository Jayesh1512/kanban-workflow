# Integrate Outreach as a persistent AntSeed Kanban

Build an **Outreach** surface in the running AntSeed application, backed by PostgreSQL and projected into a five-bucket native Vikunja board. Keep the existing six-lane conversation workflow. A company’s commercial relationship and a chat’s operational task are different things; neither should overwrite the other.

The resulting product has one relationship card per confirmed company, or a contact prospect when the company is unknown. Cards collect their Telegram tickets, evidenced contacts and imported email information. They show the five requested stages first, followed by the next AntSeed action and expandable evidence. The current private HTML supplies the interaction design and a labelled historical reference, not the production database.

## Assumptions and explicit choices

- “Integrate into our Kanban” means a first-class Outreach page and an actual managed **AntSeed Outreach** project in the existing Vikunja instance, linked from the current application and back to its operational board. This is an additional commercial dimension, not a replacement of the six operational lanes.
- Initially there is one active relationship per confirmed company. Distinct deals can become a later opportunity entity if needed; this implementation does not invent multiple opportunities from separate chats.
- Keep the single-user application. Add no authentication, ownership, tenancy or permission layer. Preserve the user’s business approval workflow.
- The supplied report is the available email source. There is no established live email connector, mailbox access, provider credential or refresh schedule to enable.
- Preserve the authorized historical Telegram rule and the actual configured `groupApprovalFrom` boundary. Unknown conversation starts cannot qualify for automatic placement. Do not substitute deployment time or discovery time.
- Implementation can start immediately. The business decisions described below are existing approval requirements for placements, not another approval request for building this feature.

## Commercial stage rules

Use stable database IDs `lead`, `qualification`, `negotiation`, `won`, `archived` and the exact display labels/order below. Stages describe supported relationship state, independently of workstream, reply status, urgency and operational lane.

| Stage | Required evidence | Insufficient evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lead / Prospect** | Evidenced business prospect or outbound enquiry; contact remains to be approached, has not replied, or substantive evaluation has not begun. Historical/research prospects are distinctly labelled. | A group name, membership, greeting, bot notification or internal chat alone. |
| **Qualification** | Substantive two-way fit discussion, evaluation, support during a trial, or engaged meeting preparation with a counterparty. | Only an outbound calendar invitation, an automated booking, or an unanswered cold message. |
| **Negotiation** | Concrete exchange terms, cost allocation, sponsorship, credits, commercial conditions or participation terms under discussion. | General interest or a trial without discussed terms. Negotiation does not imply acceptance. |
| **Deal Won** | Explicit accepted commercial outcome identifying the parties and agreed scope/consideration, supported by attributable evidence. | Successful API calls, technical completion, a meeting, planned paid testing, proposed credits or a sent contract. |
| **Archived** | Explicit supported decision to stop pursuing, close as lost, withdraw, or archive this relationship. Record reason and prior active stage. | Silence, waiting age, account deletion, an operational archived ticket or a soft-deleted source card. |

A fresh unanswered follow-up cannot erase earlier substantive evaluation. Qualification and Negotiation can have `waitingForReply=true`. Downgrades and reopening require affirmative evidence and the same decision mechanism as advances. Conflicting active conversations produce a review warning and a recommendation of `uncertain`, not “take the highest lane.” Won and Archived remain terminal until a supported reopening decision.

Regression examples must include: Aeon/Phala/Fere → Qualification; Bonfires sponsorship conditions, AgenticHub’s forwarded credit request and Eureka cost sharing → Negotiation. None establishes a win. Keep AgenticHub’s forwarded attribution. Hosted.ai/FlexAI/Divyam/Render/Positron indicate reported engagement with unknown final outcome; WOMBO/sikka remain historical-interest records without a claimed current response.

The six operational lanes and four workstreams remain valid and visible in expanded source history. There is no `Completed → won`, `Blocked → archived`, or `Integrations → negotiation` conversion table.

## Initial placement and future approvals

Use an explicit, versioned outreach baseline policy, separate from the old ticket placement marker. The existing initial-placement timestamp for an operational lane is not an approval of a different sales stage.

| Case | Product behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Historical Telegram conversation first started on/after 5 October 2026, before configured new-group boundary, included and qualifying under existing policy | One initial sales assessment may use the user-authorized historical-placement policy. Record `source=automation`, policy revision and evidence manifest through the ordinary decision/apply journal. Do not forge a Telegram approval. |
| New group at/after `groupApprovalFrom` | Keep existing intake approval. After intake, send a separate initial sales placement proposal to the configured approval group; no native sales task or accepted initial stage before approval. |
| Subsequent inferred stage change | Notify the approval group; await approve/reject/correct before accepted-stage movement. Preserve this for historical relationships too. |
| New group links to a company already represented | After intake, create a pending source membership and durable `source_enrollment` proposal even when its target equals the accepted company stage. Notify the approval group and await its decision before activating that source. Existing approved sources and company stage continue; the new group contributes no published contacts/actions/evidence until approved. |
| Unknown start date, older/ineligible conversation, uncertain evidence | No automatic sales placement. Keep inspectable context/provisional recommendation and ordinary operator decision. |
| Email report/research import | Store as provisional. Historical Telegram automation does not authorize email publication. An explicit operator baseline decision can accept selected import classifications; it is an audited business decision, not an installation gate. |

For a relationship with mixed sources, automatic historical placement is permitted only when every source relied on for that decision is eligible under the historical policy. Fresh/new-group evidence cannot be smuggled into an automatic decision through an old company card. Admission is not movement approval, and a rejected intake source cannot participate in classification.

A source membership has a durable first-placement obligation distinct from intake and identity linkage. Intake authorizes ingestion, linkage identifies the candidate company, and enrollment authorizes participation in that company’s sales placement. Store `pending/active/rejected/suspended` explicitly rather than treating a linked thread as active. Pending sources appear in a clearly separated review disclosure; they cannot enter normal active-company classification, contacts/actions, CSV exports or native task descriptions. Their evidence is accessible only to the narrowly scoped enrollment assessment and its review context. Rejected intake sources remain ineligible even for enrollment. Historical automatic enrollment follows the recorded per-source historical policy; the old operational initial-placement marker never satisfies it.

Add proposal kind `source_enrollment` alongside `stage_change` and `stage_reconciliation`; enrollment proposals carry the membership/generation IDs and expected versions. For a new group proposed at the existing Qualification stage, approval atomically records the decision and activates that membership once, increments membership/evidence/read-model revisions, and creates an immutable post-decision publication snapshot plus the updated-company-description intent in the same transaction. It does not increment `stage_version`, synthesize a stage move or create another company task. Repeated approval is idempotent. If the enrollment proposes a different company stage, approval activates membership and advances the accepted company stage atomically after validating the old stage/version; publication follows the ordinary journal. A concurrent company-stage change stales the enrollment proposal and requires a refreshed assessment rather than silently activating it under another stage. Reject leaves membership inactive. Correct creates a separately pending replacement. Source edits, remaps, intake exclusion and eligibility changes invalidate an undecided enrollment; reactivation or remapping cannot reuse an approval whose source/relationship/generation scope no longer matches. Active source suspension hides its derived contributions and queues reconciliation without erasing the accepted company-stage decision.

Use the existing configured control group. Do not create another group, send prospect outreach, replay all analysis or ask raviAntBot/Hermes for another inquiry. ## Decisions, concurrency and recovery

Add sales-specific proposal/decision/outbox/mutation tables instead of overloading Telegram-ticket `proposals`, whose non-null ticket FK and lane constraints express another domain. Reuse proven idempotency, locking and transport helpers; extract a shared mutation primitive only where both implementations can keep their existing tests. Do not undertake a generic workflow-engine rewrite.

A sales proposal records its kind (`source_enrollment/stage_change/stage_reconciliation`), optional source-membership/generation reference, source accepted stage/version, target, identity/evidence/action revisions, policy revision, evidence manifest/hash, origin and reason. A decision records approve/reject/correct, transport, idempotency key and actual operator/control provenance. Corrections create separately pending replacements. Initial decisions allow `sourceStage=null`; same-stage baselines must be recordable without faking a move. Keep `decision` and `apply_state` independent. On approval, atomically advance the local accepted business stage/version when the stage changes and queue its publication; same-stage enrollment instead activates only its named membership and queues details; `observedNativeStage` advances only after readback. Store both the complete pre-decision input snapshot and the complete post-decision publication snapshot, not only the stage versions, so the authorized activation does not make its own publication stale. The exact transaction and comparison rules follow below. Any newer accepted decision fences the older local publication acknowledgement; this is not a fence enforced by Vikunja. Unresolved earlier remote requests must become terminal before the newer operation starts, even when readback currently shows the new target. This differs deliberately from the old ticket lane, whose existing commit behavior is unchanged.

### Explicit decision-to-publication snapshot handoff

Add `outreach_publication_snapshots` and a monotonic relationship `publication_revision`. Each immutable snapshot belongs to a committed decision or an independently audited details-reconciliation intent. Store `decision_id`, `relationship_id`, accepted stage/`stage_version`, `identity_version`, `evidence_revision`, `actions_version`, aggregate `membership_revision`, `publication_revision`, policy/rubric/renderer versions, and exact eligible-source manifest plus hash. Manifest entries include source/thread ID, relationship mapping version, enrollment generation/version/state and active decision ID, inclusion/intake/policy versions, message/evidence versions and invalidation status. Store immutable selected contact/action/evidence references and rendered title/description payload/hash. Remote binding/projection generation is pinned separately on the prepared mutation and checked there. Delivery status, attempts and verification timestamps are not content inputs and cannot invalidate their own snapshot.

The proposal retains its **pre-decision assessment** unchanged. For enrollment, its assessment includes the active-source manifest plus exactly the named pending membership and that member’s evidence under the narrow assessment exception. It records expected relationship revisions and pending membership generation/version. Decision processing does the following in one transaction, with the documented source/relationship/proposal row locks:

1. Resolve the idempotency key first. A replay returns the original committed decision, target snapshot and publication intent; it does not compare the old proposal against today’s membership or activate/increment again. A different payload under that key conflicts.
2. Validate every captured pre-decision version, source eligibility and evidence value against current rows. Any unrelated edit, remap, exclusion, other enrollment or stage change causes a stale outcome before activation. An approval cannot silently refresh its input manifest.
3. Insert the decision; CAS only the named membership from its expected pending generation/version to active, incrementing its version and linking its active decision ID. Increment the aggregate membership and evidence revisions once. Advance stage/version only when required. Deterministically materialize newly eligible contacts/actions from the validated claim references within this transaction; increment `actions_version` if those selected actions change. No model/network work occurs here. Increment content/read-model and `publication_revision` once. Unrelated pending memberships remain excluded.
4. Derive the **post-decision target** from the resulting rows inside the same transaction. Capture the now-active membership version/decision ID and full eligible-source manifest, selected content and every resulting revision; insert its immutable snapshot and an outbox intent referencing that snapshot ID. The only differences from the pre-decision assessment are the explicitly authorized membership/stage/materialization changes above. Raw evidence/message versions, mappings and eligibility cannot change as an implicit side effect of approval. Failure to build a valid target rolls the entire transaction back.

Pre-apply compares current rows and recomputed eligible-source hash to the target snapshot tuple **exactly**, including `publication_revision`; it never uses the proposal’s pre-activation evidence counter or pending membership state. All source/mapping/eligibility writes use the same locked revision-bump discipline. No “ignore enrollment increments,” counter offset, wildcard manifest or ad hoc rebasing is allowed. Later source/content changes fence the target even when the accepted stage version is unchanged. Reconciliation may issue a new current-content snapshot under the retained accepted stage only after revalidating active-membership authorization and evidence; it requires a new proposal if the business stage or source enrollment needs another decision. It cannot mutate the approved snapshot in place.

For the concrete same-stage case, Qualification at stage version 4, evidence revision 12 and pending membership `(generation=1, version=1)` can approve to stage version 4, evidence revision 13 and active membership `(generation=1, version=2, active_decision_id=D)`. The target snapshot records those **post** values and all other resulting revisions; its intent updates details once and emits zero moves. A later evidence edit to revision 14 makes it stale despite stage version 4. An edit before dispatch prevents the write; an edit after dispatch follows the remote-effect reconciliation barrier below. Post-I/O acknowledgement also compares the target tuple and current worker token, so a superseded target cannot mark current content synchronized. A replay of D returns its original snapshot rather than incrementing to 14.

Journal projection creation/detail updates, movement and retirement, including expected project/view/binding generation, source and target bucket, task marker/ID, worker token/lease, immutable request ID and exact requested payload/hash. Permit one unresolved request per relationship/projection generation; bootstrap has the equivalent namespace-level barrier. Enforce this in a durable database guard, not merely an expiring lease. Requests with an unknown remote lifecycle keep the barrier even after their worker loses its lock or dies. A new worker may inspect and collect receipts, but cannot issue a replacement mutation while that barrier remains. Disable automatic write-request retries in the outreach HTTP client; retry decisions belong to this journal.

Vikunja receives no worker token or compare-and-swap precondition in the inspected move path (`src/vikunja/adapter.ts` sends only `task_id`). Its AbortSignal, client timeout and PostgreSQL lock cannot cancel or fence an already dispatched server operation. Therefore a GET observing the old or target state is only an observation: an earlier request may still execute later. There is no readback-only proof of exactly-once execution or safe remote ordering. Distinguish `prepared/not_dispatched`, `dispatched`, `remote_unresolved`, `terminal_response`, `verified` and `conflict`. Persist `dispatched` before the network call; a crash between recording and actual sending conservatively leaves an unresolved request.

A quarantined request is released only by durable terminal evidence: its actual endpoint response proves the synchronous operation finished, a conclusive transport failure proves no request bytes were dispatched, or an audited service-side drain proves the particular request and its queued/transactional effects cannot still execute. Error/status classes must be checked against endpoint behavior; a client timeout or generic proxy error is not terminal proof. An old worker that eventually receives a response may append an immutable request receipt using its request ID even after lease loss; it cannot apply business state, clear the guard, or acknowledge a newer worker's mutation. The current coordinator validates the receipt and then performs readback. A receipt lost with the process can leave quarantine indefinitely. Without a verified server execution bound, cancellation/fencing API, or service-side proof, elapsed time, aborts, repeated GETs and an operator “retry” button do not release it. Record any future drain proof with the request ID and deployment generation; do not add an automatic server restart or assume a maximum request duration. Safety here deliberately sacrifices availability for the affected card/namespace, while other cards remain runnable.

The apply algorithm is:

1. Claim durable bounded work. Acquire `antseed:outreach:relationship:<uuid>` session lock and a fenced worker token; source corrections and publication use the same lock discipline. Use one documented order for local writes: source/chat rows by stable ID, then affected relationship rows by UUID, then proposal rows. Source changes bump revisions under those row locks; publishers hold a relationship session lock across remote I/O and recheck source revisions in their short transactions. A source change can still occur during a request, so post-request fencing and superseded-effect reconciliation remain mandatory; row locks are never held across network I/O. Network I/O never runs inside a database transaction.
2. Under a short transaction, load the immutable post-decision publication snapshot, revalidate its accepted decision, exact target revision tuple and eligible-source manifest, and verify binding/projection generation; persist a prepared intent referencing that snapshot and exact payload hash before remote I/O. For enrollment, the expected values are the committed post-activation values, not the original proposal assessment.
3. Check the durable unresolved-request guard before any mutation. If one exists, collect lifecycle evidence/read-only observations and quarantine publication until terminal proof is validated. After that barrier clears, read the actual task/project and managed-view bucket through the grouped v2 endpoint. Missing task, foreign project or a third bucket is a conflict, not permission to recreate or overwrite.
4. If no earlier request can still execute and the target is already observed, reconcile it. If the source remains and inputs are current, persist dispatch intent and issue the exact approved operation once. Timeout, process death or session loss after dispatch marks `remote_unresolved`; a replacement worker must not retry, move onward or create a replacement task merely because readback shows source/target/absence.
5. After definitive completion, confirm observed effect; commit through token/version CAS and release the request guard in the same transaction. A stale worker may append its receipt but cannot acknowledge a newer worker's result. If current accepted state has advanced, record the old effect as superseded and only then enqueue the latest accepted publication. Publish remote verification timestamps separately from decision time.

Source changes after preparation need special care: if no remote effect can have happened, mark stale and recompute. If acceptance is possible or the target is observed, retain the journal and reconcile first. Record that the remote result belonged to a superseded decision; preserve the request barrier until its lifecycle is terminal, then queue a fresh correction proposal from current evidence. Readback alone cannot make this safe to release. Never delete the journal, pretend the effect was undone or blindly move back. Same rules apply to description writes; source changes queue the newer description and an older worker cannot mark it ready.

Record accepted local business decisions durably even when Vikunja is down. The page shows accepted stage, last observed native stage and pending synchronization. Telegram outages retain pending notifications; AI outages retain dirty work without fallback guesses; database outages show unavailable rather than an empty successful board. Schedule bounded retries for reads and definitively retryable operations with fair ordering so one missing or quarantined task cannot starve the queue. Expose unresolved request ID, age, last observation and required terminal evidence in status; never silently convert quarantine into success.
