# Antseed for Creators — Slide Structure

**Audience:** a top 1% content creator (runs a team, ships daily, is paranoid about being out-produced).
**Goal of the deck:** make them feel the ceiling, then hand them an unfair advantage, then make the first step tiny.
**Arc:** Tension → Shift → Reward → Close.

Pointers referred to as A1–A3, B4–B7, C8–C11, D12–D15, E16–E17, F18–F20, G21–G25.

---

## ACT 1 — TENSION

### Slide 1 — Cold open: "Your limit is not your talent."
- **Purpose:** change the frame in one line before any product talk.
- **Content:** the reframe — talent is now common; output speed is the difference. Your competitor has the same models; they do not have your pipeline.
- **Pointers:** A1, A2, A3.

### Slide 2 — The problems of the top 1%
- **Purpose:** show them their own week back to them.
- **Content:** the pain list, compressed into four lines:
  1. You are not out of ideas. You are out of credits, fast hours, and queue time.
  2. Eight subscriptions, eight logins, eight bills.
  3. Your freelancers share logins; you see the cost after the work.
  4. A new model means a new integration — and your pipeline breaks.
- **Pointers:** problems list (limits, many bills, team control, competition).

---

## ACT 2 — SHIFT

### Slide 3 — Every model. The day it drops.
- **Purpose:** sell access and speed.
- **Content:** one key for 60+ video, 35 image, voice. Kling, Veo, Seedance, Runway, Wan, MiniMax, Grok, LTX + image + TTS. New model releases → you use it the same day.
- **Pointers:** B4, B6, B7.

### Slide 4 — Never throttled. Never stuck.
- **Purpose:** remove the fear of a deadline fail.
- **Content:** many providers compete for your request; a provider fails → request moves to another; no single vendor's quota; render at 2am before launch.
- **Pointers:** B5, C11.

### Slide 5 — One key. Mix any model.
- **Purpose:** sell craft and model choice.
- **Content:** Veo for the hero shot, Kling for action, Wan for b-roll, Seedream for the thumbnail, ElevenLabs for voice — one key, one workflow.
- **Pointers:** C8.

### Slide 6 — Iterate like a studio.
- **Purpose:** the core economic reframe — quality comes from volume of iteration.
- **Content:** low cost lets you make ten times more versions and ship the best. Price table lives here as proof, not as the headline.
- **Pointers:** C9, C10, E16, E17.

### Slide 7 — Your team. One bill. Total control.
- **Purpose:** win the operator, not just the artist.
- **Content:** a key per editor and per project; budgets and caps; usage per person; one consolidated bill; no vendor lock-in.
- **Pointers:** D12, D13, D14, D15.

---

## ACT 3 — REWARD & CLOSE

### Slide 8 — Get paid to ship.
- **Purpose:** add the upside beyond saving money.
- **Content:** usage earns ANTS; referrals earn more; co-branded launch and featured spot; your audience becomes your distribution.
- **Pointers:** F18, F19, F20.

### Slide 9 — Prove it, then ask.
- **Purpose:** reverse the risk and make the first step tiny.
- **Content:** we render your shot in front of you; compare the bill. Then: get one key → make one video → post the result. Founder credibility: we build the network, we do not resell one lab.
- **Pointers:** G21, G22, G23, G24, G25.

---

## Flow check

- **Moves from emotion to logic to action:** you feel the ceiling (1–2), you see the shift (3–7), you get the upside and a tiny first step (8–9).
- **Price appears only in Slide 6**, as proof of iteration power — never as the headline.
- **Operator concerns (team, budget, bill) get their own slide (7)** so a studio leads, not just a solo artist.
- **Two questions closed on stage:** "Will I ship more / better?" (3–6) and "What is in it for me beyond cost?" (8).
